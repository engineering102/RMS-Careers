# Presentation preparation, HR interviews and group discussions

Next week your team presents to an evaluation panel, and each of you faces HR and technical interviews. Both are
learnable skills. This session gives you structures to practise this week.

## Part 1: the Demo Day presentation

Demo Day gives each team **15 minutes**: presentation plus live demo, followed by Q&A and an individual viva.

### Slide outline (about 8–10 slides)

| # | Slide | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title: product name, one-line description, team names | 0:30 |
| 2 | Problem: who suffers, what goes wrong today (a concrete example) | 1:00 |
| 3 | Solution and must-have features | 1:00 |
| 4 | Architecture diagram and tech stack, with one reason for each main choice | 1:30 |
| 5 | Data design: ER diagram or data pipeline | 1:00 |
| — | **Live demo** of the main journey (each owner presents their feature) | 6:00 |
| 6 | Quality: tests, security checklist, deployment and CI | 1:00 |
| 7 | Challenges and what we learned (one real problem and how you solved it) | 1:00 |
| 8 | Future scope (features after the freeze go here) | 0:30 |
| 9 | Thank you: live URL, repository, contributions | 0:30 |

Slide rules: one idea per slide, large readable text, diagrams over paragraphs, no code walls. Your slides support the
story; the demo proves it.

### The demo script

Write the exact clicks and words, with an owner for each part. Include the **backup plan**:

- the recorded demo video ready to play if the network or host fails;
- demo accounts already logged in on separate browser profiles, so you do not type passwords in front of the panel;
- data seeded the morning of Demo Day.

### Q&A rehearsal

Panels ask about decisions, not just features. Prepare and rehearse answers to questions like:

- Why this database? Why this architecture?
- How do you stop a user from seeing another user's data?
- What happens if two people update the same record at once?
- How did you test this? What is not tested?
- What would you do differently if you started again?
- (To each member) Walk us through the code for your feature.

Answer the question asked, briefly, then stop. If you do not know, say so and explain how you would find out. Never
answer for a teammate when the question is addressed to them.

**Dry run:** present the full 15 minutes to another team with a timer, and ask them for three pieces of feedback.

## Part 2: HR interviews

The HR round checks communication, motivation, attitude and fit. There are no trick answers, but there are clear
structures.

### "Tell me about yourself" (60–90 seconds)

Use **present → past → future**:

```text
Present: "I'm a final-year ECE student at <college>, focusing on full stack development."
Past:    "Over the last six months I built five projects, including a deployed placement
          portal where I owned the eligibility engine and the CSV export, using React,
          Express and PostgreSQL."
Future:  "I'm looking for a software engineering role where I can work on backend systems,
          which is why this position interests me."
```

Keep it relevant to the job. Your hobbies, family details and school marks belong only if they support the story.

### STAR for behavioural questions

Questions such as "Tell me about a conflict in your team" or "Describe a time you failed" are answered with **STAR**:

| Step | Content | Share of answer |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | Context in one or two sentences | short |
| **T**ask | Your responsibility | short |
| **A**ction | What **you** did, step by step | most of the answer |
| **R**esult | The outcome, with a number if possible, and what you learned | short |

Build a **story bank** of five true stories from your capstone and mini projects: a conflict, a failure, a tight
deadline, helping a teammate, learning something new quickly. One story can answer several questions.

### Common HR questions

| Question | What they want to know |
|---|---|
| Why do you want to join us? | You researched the company and role |
| What are your strengths and weaknesses? | Self-awareness; a real weakness plus what you are doing about it |
| Where do you see yourself in a few years? | Ambition that fits the role |
| Are you willing to relocate or work shifts? | Honest constraints, said politely |
| Do you have any questions for us? | Interest: ask about the team, training, or what success looks like in the first months |

### Salary-expectation etiquette

For campus hiring, the package is usually fixed and announced in advance, so the question is rare. If asked:

- Know the published package for the role before the interview.
- A respectful answer: "I understand the package for this role is fixed for campus hires, and I'm comfortable with it.
  My priority is learning and growing in the role."
- Off-campus, research typical ranges for the role and city from reliable sources, and give a range you can justify.
- Never lie about a current or competing offer.

## Part 3: group discussions (GD)

Some recruiters use a GD of 8–12 candidates for 10–15 minutes on a topic such as "Should AI tools be allowed in
exams?". Evaluators look for content, communication, listening and leadership, not volume.

**Do:**

- Take 30 seconds to note two or three points with an example each.
- Open or summarise if you can do it well; both are noticed.
- Build on others: "Adding to Priya's point about cost…".
- Bring in quieter members: "We haven't heard from Arjun yet."
- Use facts you are sure of; if unsure, argue from reasoning instead.

**Don't:** interrupt repeatedly, speak only once at the start and then go silent, get personal, or repeat what was just
said.

## Company-specific preparation sheet

For each company you apply to, keep one page: what the company does, the role description, its usual selection
process (from the company's own careers page or your placement cell), the skills it asks for, and two projects from
your portfolio that match. Review it the night before.

## Lab: dry run presentation

1. Build the slide deck from the outline and write the demo script with owners.
2. Run a timed 15-minute dry run for another team and collect feedback.
3. Each member writes a 60–90 second "tell me about yourself" and two STAR stories, and practises them in pairs.
4. Take part in the HR mock interview and the group discussion practice.

## Check yourself

1. Why should the backup plan include the recorded demo video?
2. Which STAR step should take most of your answer, and why?
3. In a GD, two members are arguing loudly and one person has not spoken. What could you say that helps the group and
   is noticed positively?
